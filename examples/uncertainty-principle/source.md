# The Uncertainty Principle

In Heisenberg's uncertainty principle, a particle's position and momentum cannot both be known with arbitrary precision at the same time.

## What it actually says

The more precisely you measure a particle's position, the less precisely you can know its momentum. This is not a statement about clumsy instruments. It is an intrinsic property of nature, rooted in the wave nature of matter.

- Measuring position more sharply widens the momentum spread
- The product of the two uncertainties has a hard floor: Planck's constant divided by four pi
- The limit applies to all conjugate pairs, not just position and momentum

## Why it matters

The uncertainty principle sets the scale of atoms. Electrons cannot fall into the nucleus because that would pin down both position and momentum at once. The shapes of chemistry, and therefore of life, are consequences of this single rule.
